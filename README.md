One picture of one day: a newspaper front page for Saturday 21 November
2026. The lead story is Russia's overnight assault on Ukraine — more
than 400 drones and 110 missiles, at least eight killed in Kyiv, and
two missiles down in Poland, which Prime Minister Tusk called a
deliberate provocation. Across the top row: an AI sell-off pushes the
S&P 500 into correction (Nasdaq −5.3%, Anthropic −11% a day after its
record debut); Bitcoin falls below $60,000 for the first time since
July, touching $57,700 as some $1.9 billion of leveraged bets are
liquidated; and the International Court of Justice finds Myanmar
committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time a state has
been held responsible. Below: Trump defends his ties to Putin and Xi
after the Shenzhen summit; a trial pill, daraxonrasib, makes pancreatic
tumours undetectable in one in 12 patients; COP31 runs into overtime
in Antalya with a draft that omits fossil fuels; a magnitude 6.7
earthquake shakes central Peru; and Britain and the EU sign their
biggest post-Brexit deals. In brief: Saudi defences intercept three
Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh, BepiColombo reaches Mercury orbit
after eight years, and a Los Angeles jury convicts the man who started
the Palisades fire.

Every headline and figure was cross-checked on the day against at
least two reporting sources (Reuters, BBC, France 24, CBS News, The
Guardian, Al Jazeera, Cointelegraph, Bloomberg).

`main.py` copies the picture to `/output/entry.png` and verifies the
copy byte for byte against a pinned SHA-256. No network, no clock, no
randomness — run it twice and the two outputs are identical.

The picture itself is drawn by `render_frontpage.py` (kept out of this
repository) from the fixed headlines above, using only the DejaVu fonts
installed on the machine, so the render is byte-identical across runs.
